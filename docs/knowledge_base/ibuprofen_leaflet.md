# Ibuprofen Tablets: Product Leaflet

## About This Medicine

Ibuprofen belongs to a group of medicines called non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs). It is used for the short-term relief of mild to moderate pain, such as headache, dental pain, muscle and joint pain and period pain, and for the reduction of fever and inflammation. Take the tablets with or after food to reduce the chance of stomach upset. Keep the tablets in their original packaging at room temperature, out of sight and reach of children, and do not use them after the expiry date printed on the pack.

## Contraindications

Do not take ibuprofen if any of the following applies:

1. You have an active stomach or peptic ulcer, a history of recurrent peptic ulcers, or gastrointestinal bleeding.
2. You have a known hypersensitivity to ibuprofen, or you have had asthma, wheezing or an allergic reaction triggered by aspirin or other NSAIDs.
3. You are in the third trimester of pregnancy.

If any of these applies to you, do not take this medicine. Ask a pharmacist or doctor for a suitable alternative. If you are unsure whether a condition applies to you, ask a pharmacist or doctor before you take the first tablet.

## Drug Interactions

Ibuprofen may interact with other medicines. Tell a pharmacist or doctor about every medicine you take, including prescription, over-the-counter and herbal products.

Ibuprofen may interact with medicines used to treat high blood pressure (antihypertensives). It may make them work less well and may increase the risk of kidney problems.

Ibuprofen should not be taken with aspirin or other NSAIDs unless a doctor has said so, and it may increase the risk of bleeding when taken with medicines that thin the blood.

Do not combine ibuprofen with any other medicine without advice from a pharmacist or doctor.
